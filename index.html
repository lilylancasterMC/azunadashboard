Target usage climbs steadily with income — 54% under $20k to 78–82% above $100k — and skews toward 18–44 year-olds, with usage barely differing by gender. Bath & Body Works is nearly flat across income (47–56% in every bracket — a genuinely mass-affordable habit), skews younger still, and is sharply female: 60% of women versus 42% of men. That's the clearest home-scent shopper template in the data; Target is the higher-income, broader-reach store already selling to a version of that same shopper.

The Recommendation

Lead with Target. Bench the TJX cluster for later.

Primary channel Target

Target reaches a higher-income, younger, design-forward shopper at real scale — the buyer who can absorb an emerging premium price point instead of defaulting to the cheapest candle on the shelf. Its own merchandising posture (rotating collaborations, a running appetite for newness in home and beauty) fits an emerging brand better than Walmart's larger but more price-driven base.

Bath & Body Works isn't a realistic wholesale channel — it overwhelmingly sells its own house brand — but it's the psychographic template Azuna Fresh should design its Target shelf presence around: young, female-skewing, habitual. The T.J. Maxx / Marshalls / HomeGoods cluster is worth a secondary push once volume matters: the audience is more price-sensitive (HomeGoods carries the group's weakest favorability, at 43.6) but the treasure-hunt format rewards exactly the rotating, small-batch SKUs an emerging brand can supply without a large production run.

Sustainability & The Basket

A value, not yet a filter

57% of US adults say they try to shop sustainably in general. Ask specifically about beauty purchases — the category closest to home fragrance — and only 31% call sustainable packaging "very" or "extremely" important. That gap is the headline: sustainability is real, but it isn't yet what decides a beauty or fragrance purchase the way scent, price, or occasion does.

Split by who's shopping, the pattern sharpens. Willingness to shop sustainably is highest among 18–44 year-olds and fades with age, and rises — unevenly, but rises — with household income. Both cuts point at the same household Target already over-indexes on.

The Metric

What ICS measures

Morning Consult's Index of Consumer Sentiment follows the same five-question methodology as the University of Michigan index: current and expected personal finances, 12-month and 5-year business-condition expectations, and current buying conditions for major household purchases, averaged into one score. It's best read as a change-over-time signal within an audience, and more cautiously as an absolute-level comparison across very different audiences — the gap below is large enough, and this consistent across all twelve months, that it's a real signal rather than noise.

The Headline Gap

A consumer sentiment reading Azuna rarely has to worry about

Azuna's core-consumer proxy (parents/guardians 18–44, HHI $100k+) has posted an ICS of 132–148 every single month for the past year — 45 to 60 points above the national reading, which itself slipped from 93.8 to 87.6 over the same period. This lines up with the spending data on the Core Consumer tab: a household spending 2.2× the national average is also one that feels considerably more financially secure.

Azuna core-consumer proxy: n ≈ 5,600–8,100 weighted/month. National: n ≈ 116,000–170,000 weighted/month.

What this means for Azuna: the core consumer Azuna is built around is not economically stretched the way the average shopper — at Target, Walmart, or even Bath & Body Works — currently is. National and retail-shopper sentiment have been softening through 2026; Azuna's target household hasn't shown the same erosion. That's a reason to lead with a premium price point and new-product launches rather than discounting defensively, though it's worth re-checking this gap periodically rather than assuming it holds indefinitely.
